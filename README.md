## What Is Data Recovery?

Data recovery is the process of getting files back from storage devices that can no longer be accessed normally.

Data loss can happen because of hardware failure, accidental deletion, file system corruption, firmware problems or other issues.

Recovery is not always possible. The result depends on the type of failure and the condition of the device.

## Hard Drive (HDD) Recovery

Hard drives can fail because of mechanical problems, damaged read/write heads, bad sectors or electronic faults.

If your hard drive starts clicking, makes unusual noises or suddenly disappears from your computer, stop using it. Repeated attempts to access a damaged drive can make the situation worse.

## SSD Data Recovery

SSDs work differently from traditional hard drives. They use flash memory and a controller to manage stored data.

Common SSD problems include:

- Controller failure
- NAND flash memory problems
- Firmware errors
- Electronic damage
- The SSD not being detected by the computer

A faulty SSD may still contain data, even if the computer cannot recognise it. Recovery depends on the type of failure and the condition of the memory.

## RAID Data Recovery

RAID systems combine multiple drives into one storage system. They are often used in servers, workstations and business environments.

Common RAID problems include:

- One or more failed drives
- RAID controller failure
- Lost or damaged RAID configuration
- Corrupted file systems
- Accidental reconfiguration

RAID 0 is especially vulnerable because it has no redundancy. If one member fails, the complete array may become inaccessible.

Do not recreate or rebuild a failed array before understanding what went wrong.

## What To Do When a Drive Fails

If your files are important, these basic steps can help avoid further damage:

1. Stop using the affected drive.
2. Do not format or initialise it.
3. Avoid writing new data to the device.
4. Do not repeatedly power on a drive that makes unusual noises.
5. Keep the original drives and their order identified in a RAID system.
6. Consider professional advice before attempting repairs.

These steps cannot guarantee recovery, but they can help preserve the available recovery options.

## Important: RAID Is Not a Backup

RAID can improve performance or provide redundancy, depending on the configuration. It does not replace an independent backup.

Keep separate copies of important files and test your backups regularly.
